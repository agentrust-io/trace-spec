    NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5 and DoD AI Tailoring Guide Cross-walk¶

    US federal and Department of Defense systems are authorized under the Risk Management Framework (RMF). An assessor preparing an authorization package asks, for each security control, what evidence shows it is in place. This page says, for each claim in a TRACE v0.2 Trust Record, which SP 800-53 Rev 5 controls that claim is evidence for, what the evidence establishes, and what it does not. It is for ISSOs, assessors, and authorizing-official staff. TRACE records what happened; it does not implement a control.

    Non-normative. This document is informative only. Nothing here changes TRACE v0.2 schema fields, wire formats, required claims, or conformance requirements. References to "TRACE" mean the TRACE v0.2 Trust Record as defined in spec/trace-v0.2.md. Control identifiers are from NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5. Tailoring Guide references are to the DoD CIO AI Cybersecurity Risk Management Tailoring Guide, version 2, 14 July 2025, with the selected controls and guidance in Tables 1-2, 2-2, 3-2 and Section 3.

    Purpose¶

    The Tailoring Guide supports control selection and tailoring for AI systems. Relevant entries include integrity (Table 2-2, printed p. 37), provenance (Table 1-2, p. 30), audit protection (Table 2-2, p. 32), and authorization-package evidence (§3.1.3, p. 16; §3.1.5, p. 20). This page suggests bounded TRACE evidence mappings; the assessor determines applicability and adequacy. Its mappings do not establish control implementation or authorization.

    A Trust Record is evidence for a control, not an implementation of it. The assessor still judges whether the evidence is adequate for the system's impact level. Where a claim's assurance depends on verified attestation, the row says so: naming a TEE in runtime.platform does not itself establish hardware backing, and a software-only record (§3.1.1) carries software commitments without hardware evidence. See trust levels.

    Field names are exact TRACE v0.2 claim names from §3.1 and the §3.2 wire-format example. Sub-field references such as model.weights_digest, policy.bundle_hash, runtime.measurement, appraisal.status, and references.rel refer to the nested fields shown there and in schema/trace-claim.json.

    Mapping table¶

    TRACE claim SP 800-53 Rev 5 controls Evidence TRACE can provide Limitations / what the assessor still needs Tailoring Guide reference
    subject IA-2, IA-9, CM-8 A workload identity (SPIFFE SVID or DID URI) names the agent, tool, or model invocation the record is about; with cnf it binds that identity to the record-signing key. Supports non-person-entity identification (IA-9) and a component inventory entry (CM-8). Identity is asserted by the issuing domain. Which permissions that identity holds in external systems is outside the record. Table 3-2 CM-8, printed p. 40
    model (model_id, version, weights_digest, aibom_uri) SI-7, SI-7(1), SI-7(15), SR-4, SR-4(3), CM-14 model.weights_digest is a digest of the model artifact that ran, so a later integrity check (SI-7(1)) or an authenticity check against a signed manifest (SI-7(15), CM-14) has a value to compare. model.aibom_uri points at a bill of materials for SR-4 provenance. The record commits to the digest; verifying that the digest matches an approved, signed artifact is the assessor's step. aibom_uri is a pointer, not attested content. Table 2-2 SI-7, p. 37; Table 1-2 SR-4/SR-4(3), p. 30; §3.1.3 SBOM
    runtime (platform, measurement, rim_uri) SI-7(9), SI-7(17) (conditional), SC-39, SR-11 runtime.measurement is the measured-boot or workload composite digest; with verified attestation evidence and appraisal.platform_measurement (§3.1.5) it establishes which layers ran what. Supports boot integrity (SI-7(9)), process isolation (SC-39), and component authenticity (SR-11). Only when attestation evidence is verified. A software-only record establishes none of these; a TEE name alone establishes nothing. Layers not listed as established in appraisal.platform_measurement are not established. SI-7(17) additionally needs verified evidence of runtime application self-protection mechanisms that detect and block exploitation; a TEE measurement alone does not establish that capability. §2 "protect the integrity ... of AI systems"
    policy (bundle_hash, enforcement_mode, version) AC-3, AC-6, CM-6, CM-7, SI-10 policy.bundle_hash names the policy set bound to the execution; policy.enforcement_mode states whether it was enforced, advisory, silent, or merely declared. Supports configuration-settings evidence (CM-6) and, for enforce, access-enforcement evidence (AC-3). declared is not evidence that any rule was checked (schema text). silent means denies were allowed. The assessor must read the mode, not just the hash. Table 3-2 AC-3/CM-6, printed pp. 39 to 40
    data_class AC-16, RA-2 The classification label the producer applied to inputs and outputs of the execution. Supports security-attribute (AC-16) and categorization (RA-2) evidence. States the label applied, not which data was read, written, or egressed. §3.1.1 data origin and reliability
    tool_transcript (hash, call_count, transcript_uri) AU-2, AU-3, AU-12, AC-6(9), SI-4 tool_transcript.hash is a commitment to the instrumented MCP/A2A transcript and tool_transcript.call_count bounds observed call volume. Supports audit-event (AU-2, AU-12) and privileged-function logging (AC-6(9)) evidence at protocol boundaries. A commitment, not a verbatim log. Calls inside the binary that never crossed an instrumented boundary are not captured. §3.1.3 AU-6 auditing
    origin (kind, producer) AU-3(1), AU-10 Names who assembled the record when it was not the executing runtime. Lets an assessor distinguish self-produced evidence from a third-party-control-plane or log-import assembly. A non-self origin MUST carry runtime.platform: "software-only" (§3.1.1); the assessor should treat such records as operator-asserted. No specific reference cited
    references (rel, id, resolver, digest) CA-2, CA-6, SA-11 (as pointers only) A condition-appraisal reference points at an independent check's finding (a test run); an authorized-intent reference points at an authorization decided before execution; an approval-outcome reference points at a human approval. These let a record be tied to TEVV results and authorization artifacts the package holds elsewhere. A reference is a pointer, not evidence (§3.1.2 rule 3). The referenced object must be obtained and verified separately; TRACE v0.2 does not define its format. See "What TRACE does not evidence" below. §3.1.3, §3.1.5 TEVV results in the package
    reproducibility (function, code_identity, input_closure, transcript_digest) with appraisal.re_execution SA-11, CA-2; SI-7(6) (conditional) Where present and re-executed by a verifier with outcome reproduced (§3.1.4), establishes that the named deterministic coordination function over the pinned closure yields the recorded transcript. Supports independent re-performance evidence. not-attempted is neither pass nor fail. Establishes nothing about model calls, which are inputs, or about side effects. Re-performance is not static code analysis (SA-11(1)). SI-7(6) additionally needs verified cryptographic protection of the pinned artifacts and closure to detect unauthorized changes; a reproduced transcript alone does not establish that mechanism. No specific reference cited
    build_provenance (slsa_level, builder, digest, provenance_depth) SA-10, SA-11, SA-15, SR-3, SR-4, CM-14 SLSA provenance for the running code and model: who built it, from what, at what level. appraisal.provenance_depth_verified records how deep the verifier checked. Supports configuration-management (SA-10), development-process (SA-15), and supply-chain (SR-3, SR-4) evidence. Depth claimed is not depth verified; read appraisal.provenance_depth_verified. Transitive dependencies are covered only at transitive depth. §3.1.3 SBOM; §3.1.5 acquisition source
    appraisal (status, verifier, method, provenance_depth_verified, platform_measurement) CA-2, CA-7, RA-5 The verifier's EAR judgment (affirming, warning, contraindicated, none) with the named verifier. Supports assessment (CA-2) and continuous-monitoring (CA-7) evidence when the verifier is independent of the operator. An appraisal by the operator's own verifier is self-assessment. none means no appraisal was performed. §3.1.5 cybersecurity T&E results
    transparency AU-9, AU-9(3) A SCITT receipt URI for an append-only log entry. When the verifier obtains and checks the inclusion proof against a log or checkpoint it trusts, supports tamper-evident audit protection (AU-9(3)). In v0.2 the claim is a URI reference, not proof of inclusion. Without a verified inclusion proof it establishes only that the producer named a log. Inclusion at a checked checkpoint does not establish an audit retention period. Table 2-2 AU-9(3)
    cnf and the record signature (§3.2.1, §3.2.2) AU-10, SC-12, SC-13, SC-17 The record is signed over its RFC 8785 canonical form by the cnf key; schema validity alone confers no trust and unbound records are rejected. Supports non-repudiation (AU-10) and cryptographic-protection (SC-13) evidence. Hardware-held keys, when attestation is verified, support SC-12 key-management evidence. Non-repudiation is as strong as the key's custody and the verifier's trust in it. Operator-held software keys bind the record to the operator, not to hardware. Table 3-1, mitigation 3.1, printed p. 39: cryptographic protections consistent with DoDI 8500.01
    iat, freshness window and clock-skew rules (§3.2.2) AU-8 Issued-at time and the spec's default 24-hour freshness and 5-minute skew tolerance give the assessor a bounded time of issuance. iat is the producer's clock; the record carries no evidence that it was synchronized with an authoritative time source (SC-45(1)). See verifying after the freshness window. No specific reference cited
    TraceRevocation/1.0 statements (§3.2.3) IA-5, IA-5(2) (conditional), SC-12, SC-17 (conditional) Log-ordered revocation of record-signing keys; a verifier reports the revocation-check outcome. Supports key-lifecycle evidence. A missing revocation bundle is reported as unchecked, not as pass. The revocation-cache mapping is to the PKI-specific IA-5(2)(b)(2); this and the SC-17 certificate context apply where the signing key is presented with an x5c chain (§3.2.1). A bare cnf.jwk does not establish that PKI context; IA-5(2)(a) separately covers public-key authentication. No specific reference cited
    delegation (parent_record_hash, credential_id) (§3.1.3) AC-2(7), AC-6, AU-3(1) Lets a verifier walk an offline-verifiable delegation chain across agents and see which parent record and credential each hop acted under. Supports privileged-account and least-privilege evidence across hops. Only hops that issued a record carrying delegation are in the chain. No specific reference cited

    What TRACE does not evidence¶

    The table marks per-claim limits. Three apply across the whole record and matter most to an authorization package.

    TRACE does not carry TEVV results. The Tailoring Guide recommends including model test and evaluation results, acquisition source, training background, and cybersecurity T&E results in the authorization package (§3.1.3, §3.1.5). A Trust Record proves what model ran, under what policy, in what environment. It does not state whether that model was tested, what the result was, or whether an official accepted the risk. §2.4 of the spec makes output correctness a permanent scope boundary. A v0.2 record can point at such results through references with rel condition-appraisal or authorized-intent, but the referenced object's format and verification are outside v0.2. An assessor citing a Trust Record for SA-11, CA-2, or CA-6 is citing the pointer, not the result.

    TRACE does not implement a control. Every row above is evidence that a condition held for one execution. Whether the control is implemented across the system boundary, for every execution, is a CA-7 continuous-monitoring question that one record cannot answer. A complete chain of records, each verified, with a trusted transparency log, is the shape of that answer; a single record is not.

    Operator-held keys bind to the operator. Where runtime.platform is software-only, or attestation is not verified, the signature establishes that the operator's key signed the record. For AU-10 non-repudiation against the operator, the assessor needs either verified hardware key custody or an independent transparency log the operator cannot rewrite.

    Suggested citation form¶

    For an authorization package or an assessment-results artifact:

    Evidence: TRACE v0.2 Trust Record, eat_profile tag:agentrust-io.com,2026:trace-v0.2, record digest sha256:… (RFC 8785 canonical form, §3.1.3 preimage), runtime.platform <value>, appraisal.status <value> by <appraisal.verifier>, transparency <URI> (inclusion proof verified: yes/no). Cited for: SI-7, SR-4, AU-9(3), AU-10, CA-7.

    The parenthetical "inclusion proof verified" records a separately checked property. The appraisal.verifier name is authenticated as that verifier only when the verifier signs the record; on a producer-signed record the name remains the producer's claim.
