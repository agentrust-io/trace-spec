# Acta decision receipts as action-receipt evidence

These fixtures are a second action-receipt profile alongside the embodied one
in the parent directory. Where that profile evidences a physical action taken
by an external controller (a robot arm, a safety monitor), this one evidences
a **software decision**: an AI agent's tool call, evaluated and decided by a
local policy gate before it runs. Same verification pattern from
[spec section 3.3.3](../../../spec/trace-v0.2.md#333-action-receipts-for-embodied-workflows-informative),
different action class, and the two should not be conflated: nothing here
claims a physical outcome.

Full field mapping and rationale: [`docs/crosswalks/acta-decision-receipts.md`](../../../docs/crosswalks/acta-decision-receipts.md).

Fixtures `01` to `06` are [draft-farley-acta-signed-receipts-02](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-farley-acta-signed-receipts-02)
envelopes (section 2.1): `{ "payload": {...}, "signature": { "alg", "kid", "sig" } }`,
with an access-decision payload per section 3.1 (`type: "protectmcp:decision"`).
Fixtures `07` to `11` are [revision 03](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-farley-acta-signed-receipts-03)
receipts in the same envelope; the two that link to a predecessor, `08` and
`09`, carry the `sha256:` link of section 6.7. Every fixture is checked against
the signer key as [`acta-keys.json`](acta-keys.json) publishes it: a JWK Set
(section 5.3) whose `valid_from` and `valid_until` give the key the validity
window of section 9.2, which only `11` falls outside. In all eleven,
`policy_digest` is an illustrative value, SHA-256 over the JCS bytes of a small
policy object, not the `acta-policy-digest-v1` manifest digest that section
6.8 of revision 03 defines; only the freshness comparison reads it.

All eleven are **real**: generated by an actual Ed25519 signer over JCS-canonical
payload bytes ([`gen.mjs`](gen.mjs) in this directory), self-checked against
[`expected.json`](expected.json) at generation time, and re-verified in CI by
[`tests/test_acta_fixtures.py`](../../../tests/test_acta_fixtures.py) and
[`tests/test_acta_receipt_verifier.py`](../../../tests/test_acta_receipt_verifier.py)
using this repository's own `rfc8785` and `cryptography` dependencies. Negative
cases are committed artifacts, not transient states inside the generator.

## Fixture cases

Expected results are machine-readable in [`expected.json`](expected.json), one
column per check in the steps below: `signing_input` (step 1, section 6.6),
`signature` (the Ed25519 check of steps 2 and 3), `key_window` (the section
9.2 window in step 3), `chain` (step 4), and `policy_freshness` and
`session_binding` (step 5). `n/a` marks a check that does not apply, such as
`chain` for a fixture with no declared predecessor, or one that is not
asserted: `03`'s signature fails, so its `issued_at`, `policy_digest` and
`session_id` are not authenticated, and the checks that read them are `n/a`.

| File | Signature | Other checks | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `01-valid-accepted.json` | pass | all pass | A safe tool call the policy permitted. First receipt in the chain (no `previousReceiptHash`). |
| `02-valid-denied.json` | pass | all pass | A destructive tool call the policy blocked, evidenced with the same rigor as an allow. A denial is not an absence of evidence, it is its own signed record. `previousReceiptHash` correctly links to `01` per section 6.7 of revision 03 (5.7 of revision 02). |
| `03-signature-key-mismatch.json` | **fail** | n/a | Signed by a real but different key ([`mismatched-signer-public-key.txt`](mismatched-signer-public-key.txt), committed) while claiming the signer's `kid`. Resolving `kid` to the published [`signer-public-key.txt`](signer-public-key.txt) deterministically fails verification. The mismatch is in the committed bytes, reproducible by any independent verifier. |
| `04-broken-chain.json` | pass | **chain fail** | Validly signed by the correct key, but `previousReceiptHash` carries the envelope hash of `01` when the receipt's actual predecessor in the chain is `02`. The signature check passes; recomputing SHA-256(JCS(predecessor envelope)) does not match. This exercises chain verification as its own check: a dishonest or buggy issuer can validly sign a wrong chain pointer, and only recomputation against the actual predecessor catches reordering or omission. |
| `05-stale-policy-digest.json` | pass | **freshness fail** | Validly signed, but `policy_digest` names a policy bundle that is no longer the one in force (`current_policy_digest` in `expected.json`). Cryptographic validity and authorization freshness are different checks; a verifier that stops at the signature accepts a receipt whose authorization basis has since changed. |
| `06-session-binding-mismatch.json` | pass | **binding fail** | Validly signed, but `session_id` belongs to a different session than the one the verifier expects (`expected_session_id` in `expected.json`). A genuine receipt replayed from another session passes every cryptographic check and fails only the binding comparison. |
| `07-valid-inside-key-window.json` | pass | all pass | A revision 03 receipt issued inside the signer key's validity window. First receipt of the revision 03 chain (no `previousReceiptHash`). |
| `08-valid-prefixed-chain-link.json` | pass | all pass | `previousReceiptHash` is `sha256:` and the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the JCS bytes of `07`'s entire envelope, the form section 6.7 of revision 03 requires of the field. |
| `09-payload-only-chain-link.json` | pass | **chain fail** | `08` with its link computed over `07`'s payload member alone. Section 6.7 (5.7 in revision 02) makes the whole signed receipt the preimage, so that re-signing a payload changes the link; a payload-only link would not change. The link is well formed and the signature valid; only recomputing the hash of the whole predecessor shows the mismatch. |
| `10-signature-member-in-payload.json` | pass | **signing input fail** | `07` with `"signature": null` added to the payload, signed over those bytes. The Ed25519 signature verifies; section 6.6 refuses a payload that carries a `signature` member, `null` and the empty string included. |
| `11-outside-key-window.json` | pass | **key window fail** | `07` dated two weeks before the key's `valid_from`. Validly signed by the right key; only the section 9.2 window check refuses it, because the key set says the key was not yet valid on the date the receipt gives. Step 3 below says how these fixtures read section 9.2. |

`04`, `05`, and `06` together make one point three ways: signature
verification proves integrity and origin, and nothing else. Chain position,
policy freshness, and session binding are separate verifier obligations with
separately observable failures.

`09`, `10`, and `11` make the same point for three rules as revision 03 states
them, in sections 6.7, 6.6 and 9.2. Revision 02 already had all three (sections
5.7, 5.6 and 8.2); revision 03 adds the `sha256:` prefix and states the signing
input for each receipt shape. Each of the three is a valid fixture (`08`, `07`,
and `07`) with one payload member changed and signed again by the right key, so
the rule that member breaks is the only check it fails. A verifier that accepts
a link over the predecessor's payload as well as one over the whole envelope
gets the expected result for every fixture here except `09`.

## Verifying independently

To check any fixture without this repo's tooling, per revision 03 sections
6.6, 6.7 and 9.2 (in revision 02, sections 4.1 and 5.6, 5.7, and 8.2):

1. Extract `payload` from the envelope, and refuse it if it carries a
   `signature` member, even `null` (section 6.6; `10` does).
2. Serialize it with JCS (RFC 8785): recursively sorted keys, no whitespace.
3. Verify `signature.sig` as a PureEdDSA (Ed25519) signature directly over
   those bytes (no pre-hash), against the public key that `signature.kid`
   resolves to: [`signer-public-key.txt`](signer-public-key.txt) (hex), or
   the same key in [`acta-keys.json`](acta-keys.json), which also gives its
   validity window: `payload.issued_at` must fall inside it (section 9.2;
   `11` does not). Section 9.2 is a SHOULD, and it names neither the time to
   compare with the window nor whether an end is inclusive. These fixtures
   compare `issued_at`, the time the receipt gives, as archival verification
   (section 9.1) would, and every fixture is dated at least a day from both
   ends. A verifier that compares the time of verification instead, or skips
   the check, accepts `11` and gives every other fixture its expected result,
   since `valid_until` is in 2036.
4. Chain check (revision 03 section 6.7): `previousReceiptHash` must name
   the SHA-256 of the JCS bytes of the predecessor's **entire envelope,
   signature included**. Revision 03 writes it as `sha256:` followed by the
   lowercase hex, as `08` and `09` do; `02` and `04` carry the bare hex of
   revision 02, which section 6.7 still lets a verifier accept. The
   predecessor for each chained fixture is declared in `expected.json`.
5. Freshness and binding checks: compare `policy_digest` and `session_id`
   against `current_policy_digest` and `expected_session_id` in
   `expected.json`.

[`tools/acta_receipt_verifier.py`](../../../tools/acta_receipt_verifier.py)
runs steps 1 to 4 on one receipt, with the issuer key and its window taken
from a JWK Set such as `acta-keys.json`.
[`tests/test_acta_fixtures.py`](../../../tests/test_acta_fixtures.py) uses the
verifier's signature and chain helpers for steps 3 and 4 (the helpers apply
neither the section 6.6 rule nor a window), makes the section 6.6, window and
step 5 comparisons itself, and checks that each of `09`, `10` and `11` changes
one payload member of a valid fixture and fails only the column that member
breaks;
[`tests/test_acta_receipt_verifier.py`](../../../tests/test_acta_receipt_verifier.py)
runs the whole verifier over every fixture under `acta-keys.json`, and covers
the window boundaries and the other link forms with synthetic receipts.

Expected outcomes per fixture are in `expected.json`; the tests check every
column that is not `n/a` against the fixture itself, so fixture or envelope
drift fails the build rather than passing silently.

## Boundary

Same boundary the embodied profile states, generalized: a valid Acta receipt
proves a specific policy decision, over a specific tool call, was signed by
the operator's gate at a specific time, and that the record has not been
altered since. It does not prove the tool call's real-world side effect was
safe, correct, or reversible, and (per `04`/`05`/`06`) signature validity
alone does not prove chain position, policy freshness, or session binding.
